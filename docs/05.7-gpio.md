# GPIO

The ten routable GPIOs (`GPIO 1`-`8` plus the UART `Tx` and `Rx` pins) can now be set up from the board itself, no terminal needed - direction, pulls, PWM, and a binary counter that drives a group of them as one number. All of it renders on the OLED and the terminal only. Nothing here paints the breadboard, so the LEDs keep showing your circuit while you change what's driving it.

There are three ways in: click on a highlighted GPIO `net`, the `GPIO` click menu, or a logo pad tap for the counter.

## Highlight a GPIO and click

Land the highlight on a `net` that has a routable GPIO on it - scroll there with the `clickwheel`, or tap the `row` with the `probe`. The reading card shows the pin and its level (`GPIO 3 out` `HIGH`) with an `options?` tag on it. That tag is a 1 second window: click the `clickwheel` while it's showing and the pin's options open. Click after it's gone and you get the regular click menu, same as any other `row`. Turning the wheel is never anything but scrolling, so you can scroll straight past a GPIO `net`.

Holding the `probe` on the `row` keeps the window open - the second starts counting when you lift. Leaving the options re-highlights the same `net` and gives you one fresh window, so a quick second click gets you back in. This needs the OLED: without one there's no window, and a click just opens the menu.

If something else owns the pin, the OLED names the owner instead - `GPIO 7` / `OLED` - and nothing opens. Owners are things like `OLED` (the display bus), `python` (a script claimed it), `probe power`, `UART` (passthrough traffic since boot), and `routing`. PWM doesn't count as an owner - the editors take a PWM pin over.

### The options

The OLED shows the pin's name up top and one option below it: `< Dir  out >`, `< PWM  off >`, `< Pulls  none >`, `< BCD  off >`. Turn to move between them, click to edit one, hold to leave. `PWM` isn't offered on `Tx` / `Rx`. Every editor names the pin it's editing (`GPIO 2 Dir`, `Tx Pull`) so you can't lose track of which one you're on.

- `Dir` - turn flips `out` / `in`, click applies, hold cancels
- `Pulls` - turn cycles `down` / `up` / `none` / `keep`, click applies. On an input it takes effect right away; on an output it's stored and kicks in when the pin becomes an input
- `PWM` - turn flips `on` / `off`, click applies. Turning it `on` opens a `Freq` / `Duty` picker: click one and the wheel changes it live on the pin (frequency steps by ratio so a spin sweeps decades, duty in 1% steps). Click keeps what you landed on, hold puts the value you came in with back
- `BCD` - straight into the [counter](#the-bcd-counter)

Inside an editor, a hold backs out to the options; a hold on the options leaves them. The `probe` works too: `Connect` is click, `Remove` is hold.

## The GPIO click menu

The click menu has a `GPIO` entry with two things in it:

`GPIO` > `Set Pins` lists all ten pins with their live state on each line - `3  out HIGH`, `5  in  pull-up`, `2  PWM 1.0k`, `Tx  UART`. A pin something owns shows its owner instead (`7  OLED`) and refuses the click. Pick a pin and you get the same `Dir` / `Pull` / `PWM` / `BCD` options as above (each line shows the current value - `Dir  out`, `Pull  none`), and applying a value drops you back on that pin's option list, so you can set its direction and then its pull without re-entering anything.

`GPIO` > `BCD Counter` has `Count` and `Range`, described below.

A hold anywhere in either app walks you all the way home: it closes the whole thing and reopens the top-level click menu with `GPIO` highlighted. There's no back-one-level in the menu apps - that's what the list return after each edit is for. Typing anything in the terminal exits the app outright.

## The BCD counter

Pick some pins, and the Jumperless drives them as the bits of one number you turn up and down with the `clickwheel`. It's for things like a 7447 driving a 7-segment display, or stepping an address bus by hand. (The name stuck; the encoding is plain binary and the readout is hex - there's no decimal mode.)

Bit order is LSB-first in pin order: `GPIO 1` is the lowest bit of whatever's in the range, then up through `GPIO 8`, then `Tx`, then `Rx`. The readout is one hex digit per four pins - `0`-`F` with up to 4 pins, `00`-`FF` with 5 to 8, `000`-`3FF` with all 10 - and the count wraps in both directions.

### Counting

Get there by clicking `BCD` on a highlighted pin's options, from `Set Pins`, or with `GPIO` > `BCD Counter` > `Count`. The OLED shows every pin in the range along the top - the pin labels (`1 2 3 4`, with `T` and `R` for the UART pins) over their live levels (`0 1 0 1`, or `-` for a bit whose pin something else owns) - and the hex count big underneath, next to `BCD`. The terminal mirrors the same line.

Turn the wheel and the pins change as you go, clockwise counts up, and the `row`s those pins are wired to change level on the breadboard right along with the count. The number *is* the pins: the modal reads the value off them when it opens and keeps re-reading, so if something else moves a pin (a MicroPython script, say) the count and the levels row update in place. Click or hold both just leave - whatever's on the pins stays there, nothing reverts.

Before it opens, if no range is configured it defines one for you:

- every routed GPIO that's free to claim, in pin order - the wires you placed are the range, and they don't need to be next to each other
- nothing routed? A run of up to 4 free pins starting from the pin you were on (or the lowest free one from the `BCD Counter` menu)

If no pin is free at all it says `BCD` / `no free pins` and stays put. A pin that's only busy with PWM counts as free - the counter takes it over.

### Range

`GPIO` > `BCD Counter` > `Range` picks the pins explicitly. The first stop is `Routed` with the routed pins spelled out (`1-4`, or `1,3,T` for a sparse set) - click it and that's the range, done. With nothing routed it reads `Routed` / `none` and refuses the click. Turn past it for `Start` (`GPIO 1`... `GPIO 8`, `Tx`, `Rx` - only free pins are offered), click, then `Width` (`4 bits`), one detent per step, clamped from 1 to the end of the free run. Click commits and drives the count onto the new pins; hold cancels with nothing changed.

The `BCD Counter` menu shows the state on its lines - `Count  7` and `Range  1-4`, or `Count  no range` and `Range  off`.

### Counting with the logo pads

Any of the four pad idle actions can count. Put these in your [config](06-config.md):

```jython
`[logo_pads] top_guy_idle = bcd_up;
`[logo_pads] bottom_guy_idle = bcd_down;
```

(`building_pad_top_idle` and `building_pad_bottom_idle` take the same values.) Then, with the `probe` parked and its switch in `Select`, a tap on the top guy counts up and the bottom guy counts down - one step per touch, wrapping - and the OLED toasts `BCD` / `7` with the new value. Each tap counts from what's actually on the pins, so a pin you toggled by hand in between isn't undone. With no range configured the toast says `BCD` / `no range`.

## Choose GPIO on the OLED

Tapping the GPIO pad in probe mode brings up the `Choose GPIO` chooser on the breadboard as before - and now the OLED mirrors it: a little breadboard outline with `1`-`4` across the top and `5`-`8` across the bottom, with `i` and `o` marks at the tap zones for input and output. In `Remove` mode the `i` / `o` marks are hidden, same as on the LEDs. When a tap lands, the OLED confirms it (`GPIO 3` / `Input`), and the input/output picker shows `GPIO 3` / `Input / Output` while you choose. Picking a pin something owns names the owner (`GPIO 7` / `OLED`) and cancels the connection instead of stealing the pin.

## What's saved

Direction, pulls, and PWM settings go with the `slot` as they always have. The counter does too - its pins and current value sit in the slot YAML under `config:` as `bcd: {pins: 15, value: 5}`, where `pins` is a bitmask (bit 0 = `GPIO 1` ... bit 7 = `GPIO 8`, bit 8 = `Tx`, bit 9 = `Rx`). Loading a slot drives the saved count back onto the pins. Slot files from the earlier `start` / `width` form still load.
