# Calibration

Everything under `Calibration` in the click wheel menu, what each one actually measures, and which one to run when something's off. The numbers they produce live in `config.txt` (the `[probe]` and `[calibration]` sections), survive `` `reset `` and firmware updates, and get shown on the OLED and in the terminal while each one runs, so it's worth having a serial terminal or the app open when you calibrate.

The menu:

- [`Probe Pads`](#probe-pads) - taps land on the wrong row
- [`Switch Thresh`](#switch-thresh) - the board thinks the probe switch is in the wrong position
- [`DACs Calib`](#dacs-calib) - DAC, rail, or ADC voltages are off
- [`Full Test`](#full-test-and-the-single-tests) - the factory self test, all five parts
- [`Probe Cable`](#probe-cable), [`Xbar Route`](#xbar-route), [`Tip Voltage`](#tip-voltage), [`PSRAM Check`](#psram-check) - one part of the self test at a time

## Which one do I need?

- **Taps light the row next to the one you touched**, or rows "drift" between neighbors → `Probe Pads`
- **Rows are fine but the `ADC`/`DAC`/`GPIO`/logo pads act like the pad next to them** → `Probe Pads` (on older firmware this was a bug, not you - [see below](#the-small-pads-were-off-by-one-in-measure))
- **Taps are right on `Select` but off on `Measure`**, or the other way round → `Probe Pads`, with the switch in **both** positions
- **The logo is the wrong color for where the switch is**, `Measure` mode won't start, or the board flips modes by itself → `Switch Thresh`
- **A rail or DAC set to `3.3V` measures something else**, or ADC readings are off → `DACs Calib`
- **`Measure` mode reads a known voltage wrong** → `Tip Voltage`, then `DACs Calib` if it's still off
- **The probe LED is dark, or the buttons only work sometimes** → `Probe Cable`
- **A connection you made doesn't conduct** → `Xbar Route`
- **MicroPython runs out of memory** → `PSRAM Check`
- **Something's wrong and you don't know what** → `Full Test`

**Fingers off the pads.** The probe is read through a resistive voltage divider, so a finger on the pads, or on the back of the four risers that connect the probe sense boards to the main board, changes the readings. That goes double while calibrating.

## Probe Pads

### How the probe finds a row

Every row and pad has a little contact next to it, and all of them are strung together into one long resistor ladder. The probe tip drives a voltage into whichever contact it's touching, and the voltage that comes out the bottom of the ladder tells the board how far along the ladder you are. That's it: one ADC reading, mapped linearly onto 101 positions (rows 1-60, the rails, the nano header, the logo pads, the `ADC`/`DAC`/`GPIO` pads).

A linear map needs two endpoints, and that's all a probe calibration is:

- **The floor** (`pad_min`) - what the ADC reads with the tip in the air. It's the offset of the sense node itself, not a pad, so it's the same in both switch positions. The board measures it at every boot and again the moment you open `Probe Pads` (so keep the tip off the board for the first second).
- **The top** (`pad_max` in `Select`, `pad_max_measure` and `pad_max_measure_gpio` in `Measure`) - where row 1 lands. This is the number the clickwheel moves.

`Select` and `Measure` need separate tops because the tip is driven by different things: on `Select` it's wired straight to a pin on the RP2350; on `Measure` it's the probe's buffer, fed through the crossbar by either `DAC0` or a spare GPIO (the board picks whichever is free). The GPIO feed sags a little more under load than `DAC0` does, so `Measure` actually carries two tops, one per feed, and the app matches them against each other for you.

### Running it

Open `Calibration` > `Probe Pads` with the tip in the air. The breadboard shows `Adjust` on the left (the wheel) and `Tap` on the right (the probe), the terminal prints the floor it just measured, and the OLED shows the instructions.

<!-- shot: probe-pads-start_board.png - board LEDs right after opening Probe Pads ("Adjust" / "Tap") -->
<!-- shot: probe-pads-start_oled.png - OLED instructions -->

```jython
Probe Calibration App

Tap pads with the probe and rotate the clickwheel until the correct row lights up
Check both ends: row 1 and row 60, the nano header, and the logo / ADC / DAC / GPIO pads
The bottom end (the small pads) is set by the nothing-touched floor, measured
right now with the tip in the air - the wheel only moves the top end.
SELECT position calibrates probe_max. The feed is pinned to DAC0 there so
INA1 can measure the switch (probe LED supply) current - shown as sw:.
MEASURE position: hold the tip on ONE breadboard row for half a second and
the app samples it under both tip feeds (DAC0, then a GPIO) and sets
probe_max_measure_gpio so both decode the SAME row. After that the wheel
moves both measure endpoints together. Short-click re-runs the matching.
Hold the clickwheel when you're done

nothing-touched floor: 21 raw (was 21 at boot)
```

Then, with the switch on **`Select`** (towards the tip):

1. Tap a row near the top of the board - row 1 or 2 is best, it's where the top endpoint has the most leverage. The row the board *thinks* you tapped lights up blue.
2. If it's the wrong row, turn the wheel until the right one lights. You don't have to keep the tip on the pad - the board keeps re-decoding your last tap as you turn, so tap, lift, turn, watch.
3. Tap a few more places to check: row 60, a nano header pin, and the `ADC`/`DAC`/`GPIO` pads and the logo. The small pads light their own LEDs (the `ADC` pad lights the two ADC LEDs, and so on). If the top rows are right and the small pads are still off, the floor is wrong: lift the tip, exit, and open the app again so it re-measures.

A dim second row is the old calibration's opinion of the same tap, so you can see how far you've moved it.

<!-- shot: probe-pads-select-row1_board.png - Select position, tip on row 1, row 1 lit blue -->
<!-- shot: probe-pads-select-adc-pad_board.png - Select position, tip on the ADC pad, the ADC LEDs lit -->
<!-- shot: probe-pads-select_oled.png - the SELECT status screen -->

The OLED (and the terminal, on one line that keeps updating) shows the raw reading, the decoded position, the node, the current top endpoint and, on `Select`, the switch current `sw:` that [`Switch Thresh`](#switch-thresh) uses:

```jython
raw: 3991 enc: 0 reading: 1 max: 4055 node: 1 select sw: 1.42 mA (thr 0.90/1.20)
```

Now flip the switch to **`Measure`** (away from the tip). The highlight turns purple and the app wants one more thing from you before the wheel is useful:

1. **Hold** the tip on one breadboard row (any of rows 1-60, not the header or the small pads) for about half a second and leave it there. The status goes `hold row` → `match..` → `ok` while the board pins the tip feed to `DAC0`, takes a few readings, pins it to a GPIO, takes a few more, and works out the GPIO top that makes both feeds decode your row the same way. The row LED may blink once or twice while it does this - each feed change is a crossbar rebuild. The terminal confirms it:

        feeds matched on row 12: DAC max 4134 / GPIO max 4061 (was 4055)
        now turn the wheel to align the row (moves both)

2. Tap and turn exactly like on `Select`. The wheel now moves both `Measure` tops together so the match you just made isn't undone. A short click of the wheel re-runs the matching if you want to redo it.

3. **Hold the wheel** to save.

The terminal prints what's being written:

```jython
Probe calibration saved!
Returning to menu

floor (both positions): probe_min              = 21
select:                 probe_max              = 4055
measure (DAC0 feed):    probe_max_measure      = 4134
measure (GPIO feed):    probe_max_measure_gpio = 4061  (matched)
select switch current (DAC0 feed, INA1): 1.42 mA   thresholds 0.90 / 1.20
tip drive (fixed):      measure_mode_output_voltage = 3.310 V

```

If it adds `NOTE: that is not above the SELECT threshold - run Switch Calib.`, do that next.

### The small pads were off by one in Measure

If you're on firmware 5.7.11.1 or earlier and the `ADC`, `DAC`, `GPIO` and logo pads all read as the pad next to them - only with the switch on `Measure`, only when connecting - that's a firmware bug, not your calibration. `Measure` position used its own floor setting that nothing ever measured, so it sat at a default of 10 while the real floor (measured at boot, and used by `Select`) is typically 15-50. The bottom of the map is set almost entirely by the floor, so a gap of 40 counts is a full position at the small pads and a quarter to half a row in the middle of the board (the "rows occasionally drift" part), and no amount of turning the wheel - which only moves the top - can fix it. Update; both positions share the measured floor now. You can see your floor as `pad_min` in the [config](06-config.md) and in the first line `Probe Pads` prints.

## Switch Thresh

The probe's slide switch has no wire to the board - the board works out where it is by what the probe *draws*. On `Select` the probe LED is powered from the buffer supply, so the buffer's current (through an INA219 in the `DAC0` path) jumps by about 1.4 mA. `Switch Thresh` measures that current in both positions and puts the `Measure`/`Select` decision thresholds halfway between, with a little hysteresis. It also calibrates a second, faster detector that watches how the tip line behaves when the feed blinks off for a few microseconds (the LED's capacitor holds it up on `Select`; nothing does on `Measure`).

Run it from `Calibration` > `Switch Thresh` and follow the two prompts:

1. `Switch to MEASURE (AWAY from TIP)`, click the wheel. It takes 20 current readings (you can watch them fill in on the breadboard), then measures the tip under the GPIO feed.
2. `Switch to SELECT (TOWARDS TIP)`, click the wheel. Same again.

```jython
=== Calibration Results ===
MEASURE mode median: 0.012 mA   (tip H ok)
SELECT mode median:  1.412 mA   (tip L ok)
Midpoint: 0.712 mA
Range: 1.400 mA

Low threshold:  0.642 mA (switch to MEASURE)
```

Two things make it refuse to save: if the tip-side detector says the switch wasn't where the prompt asked (`the tip sense says the switch was in SELECT during the MEASURE step`), and if the two currents aren't at least 0.5 mA apart (`readings out of expected range ... this may mean a bad probe cable!`), in which case it keeps the defaults. Either way, any key in the terminal aborts without saving. The firmware also runs this once by itself after an update from a version that didn't have it.

## DACs Calib

The four DAC channels (`DAC0`, `DAC1`, the top rail, the bottom rail) go through a scale-and-shift stage to get their ±8 V range, and every board's is slightly different. `DACs Calib` sets each one to a series of voltages, reads them back with the INA219 (which can only see positive voltages, hence the series), and solves for the zero and full-scale numbers that make "set 3.3 V" come out as 3.3 V. Then it does the same for the ADCs, using `DAC1` as the reference: `ADC0`-`ADC3`, `ADC4` (the 0-5 V one) and `ADC7` (the probe tip). It runs by itself on first boot, and `$` in the terminal is the same thing.

**Unplug anything connected to the rails first.** It drives the rails through their range and reads them, and a load on a rail turns into a wrong calibration.

<!-- shot: dacs-calib-zero_board.png - "Zero DAC 0" on the breadboard with the progress dots -->
<!-- shot: dacs-calib-spread_board.png - "Spred" phase -->

It takes about a minute and narrates the whole thing - each channel gets a `zeroing DAC` line, a `finding spread` series, and the ADCs get a `Set / INA / raw ADC` line per point, then it all lands in one table (these are typical numbers; yours will differ a little):

```jython
	Calibration Values

            DAC Zero	DAC Spread	ADC Zero	ADC Spread
DAC 0       1650	20.51		8.12		18.28
DAC 1       1651	20.49		8.09		18.30
Top Rail    1647	20.55		8.21		18.27
Bottom Rail 1652	20.50		8.15		18.31
```

`ADC calibration failed - insufficient valid points` means the INA didn't agree with the DAC at enough points - usually something on a rail, or the USB audio stream holding the ADC (close the microphone on the host, or `M` to turn it off, and run it again).

## Full Test and the single tests

`Full Test` is the factory self test: five checks, each painted onto the breadboard as a band of green (pass) or red (fail) when it finishes, plus a human report and a one-line JSON report in the terminal, stored in `/selftest.json` (`` `self_test_report `` prints it again). Anything that fails gets re-run in rounds until it passes; any input during the 5-second countdown between rounds accepts the failing report instead. At the end it holds the result on the LEDs until you touch a probe button, the wheel, or a key, then restarts. It runs by itself at the end of the first-boot `DACs Calib`, and `` `self_test `` from the terminal is the same thing.

<!-- shot: full-test-result_board.png - the five result bands on the breadboard -->

The four entries below it run one check each and don't restart afterwards - the result stays on the LEDs until you touch something.

### Probe Cable

The most common probe failure is one contact in the TRRS jack. A good cable shorts two of the probe pins together at the jack, so this drives each of those pins and reads the other, both ways, eight times with a wiggle in between so an intermittent contact shows up as a dropped edge. It then checks that the button line floats when no button is pressed and that the probe's LED data actually reaches the probe. An open short on the spare pin is a warning, not a failure, as long as the pin the firmware actually uses works.

### Xbar Route

The CH446Q crossbar chips can't be read back, so the only way to know a crosspoint closed is to put a voltage through it. This routes `DAC1` at 2.5 V to every breadboard row in turn with a rotating `ADC0`-`ADC3` return path and measures it, then loops the routable GPIOs and both rails through the matrix the same way. A row that doesn't conduct is named in the report.

### Tip Voltage

`Measure` mode drives the probe tip from the buffer at `measure_voltage` (about 3.3 V). This servos that setting until what `ADC7` reads at the tip exactly matches what `DAC1` set to 3.300 V reads through the same ADC, and rejects the run if the tip is moving (a hand on the probe shows up as spread between bursts). It also measures the GPIO feed's droop (`droop_v0`, `droop_ohms`), which the switch detector uses when a GPIO is feeding the buffer. Nothing else should change `measure_voltage` - the pad decode cancels the tip voltage out, so it's not a knob for row alignment.

### PSRAM Check

Writes and reads back the 8 MB PSRAM (if `psram_installed` is on). This is where the MicroPython heap and the app arena live, so a failure here explains out-of-memory errors.

## Where the numbers live

All of it is in `config.txt`, editable with `` ` `` or the [config editor](06-config.md). `` `reset `` keeps these; `` `reset_calibration `` puts them back to defaults (and `` `force_first_start `` wipes everything and re-runs the first-boot calibration).

`[probe]`:

| key | set by | what |
|---|---|---|
| `pad_min` | boot, `Probe Pads` | the floor: pad ADC with nothing touched, both positions |
| `pad_max` | `Probe Pads` | top of the map on `Select` |
| `pad_max_measure` | `Probe Pads` | top of the map on `Measure`, `DAC0` feed |
| `pad_max_measure_gpio` | `Probe Pads` | same, GPIO feed |
| `min_valid_reading` | default 85 | below this raw reading = no touch |
| `switch_threshold_low` / `_high` | `Switch Thresh` | buffer mA below/above which the switch is `Measure`/`Select` |
| `switch_select_max_ma` | `Switch Thresh` | above this it's a touch loading the buffer, not the switch |
| `switch_blink_hold_pct` | `Switch Thresh` | feed-blink detector threshold (GPIO feed) |
| `current_zero` | boot | INA reading with the probe unloaded |
| `measure_voltage` | `Tip Voltage` | the `Measure`-mode tip drive |
| `droop_v0` / `droop_ohms` | `Tip Voltage`, `Switch Thresh` | the GPIO feed's droop model |

`[calibration]`: `dac_0_zero` / `dac_0_spread`, `dac_1_*`, `top_rail_*`, `bottom_rail_*`, `adc_0_*` through `adc_4_*` and `adc_7_*`, all from `DACs Calib`.
